# Lightweight 3D-guided neural rendering: research

Research snapshot: 2026-11-11. Status: source and documentation review; no local renderer build, capture, or benchmark has been run. Scores below are engineering judgments, not measured compatibility. The [architecture](ARCHITECTURE.md) and [plan](IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md) turn these findings into testable steps.

## What DLSS 5 publicly establishes

NVIDIA's [DLSS 5 research page](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/adlr/DLSS5/) (2026-09-01) describes a **one-step pixel-space diffusion** renderer conditioned at inference on the rendered color frame, engine motion vectors, carried temporal state, and artistic controls. Renderer-derived scene attributes are used for consistency supervision during training. NVIDIA calls the system causal and deterministic, with one output per input frame, and reports up to 4K on RTX 50 Series. Its [developer article](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/whats-new-for-game-developers-dlss-5-with-3d-guided-neural-rendering-nvidia-ace-updates-and-new-rtx-kit-capabilities/) (2026-09-22) places it near the end of the rendering pipeline and describes model selection, structure and tone intensity, and semantic/engine masks. These are NVIDIA claims, not independently reproduced measurements.

The [official comparison](https://www.nvidia.com/en-gb/geforce/news/dlss-5-3d-guided-neural-rendering/) separates DLSS 5 appearance generation from Super Resolution (spatial reconstruction), Frame Generation (new temporal samples), and Ray Reconstruction (reconstruction of ray-traced lighting). Classical denoising estimates a cleaner version of a sampled signal; DLSS 5 can generate appearance changes such as lighting and materials, under renderer guidance. Published FPS demonstrations combine multiple DLSS features, so they do **not** isolate DLSS 5 latency or cost.

The [linked research report PDF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/adlr/DLSS5/files/DLSS5_Report.pdf) was not inspected: the web fetch rejected its roughly 294 MB size. Exact topology, parameter count, losses, corpus, training compute, and isolated latency are **unverified**. In particular, normals shown as training guidance are not evidence that normals are a required inference input. Our proposed AO network is an educational approximation, not a DLSS 5 reproduction.

SM64 emulation is distinct from a native source port: an emulator can expose a video frame but usually hides the game's 3D semantics from a straightforward rendering hook. A native port exposes its graphics code, yet original assets must be supplied lawfully by the user. No game assets are needed for the first experiment. SuperTuxKart is the preferred **later game demonstration**, conditional on renderer inspection and an M2 build. Godot is the research testbed. A custom Metal scene is the fallback if Godot buffer/export control fails. The custom renderer scores highly because it is controllable, but its implementation cost is higher than Godot's.

## How the selected renderer produces a frame

In Godot Forward+, mesh vertex/index buffers and material data enter vertex processing; projected triangles are rasterized into pixels and depth. The Forward+ renderer uses scene geometry and lighting to shade a color buffer, applies screen-space/post effects, and presents the viewport. A `CompositorEffect` can run a rendering-thread callback at a selected `EffectCallbackType` before presentation. Exact pass order and available attachments at the chosen callback must be inspected in a GPU frame capture; this description is the documented high-level path, not an audited engine execution trace.

The [stable `CompositorEffect` API](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_compositoreffect.html) documents `_render_callback(effect_callback_type, render_data)`, `access_resolved_color`, `access_resolved_depth`, `needs_normal_roughness`, and `needs_motion_vectors`. The [RenderSceneBuffersRD API](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/classes/class_renderscenebuffersrd.html) documents `get_texture`, `get_velocity_texture`, and `get_internal_size`; buffers exist only during viewport rendering. `get_texture("render_buffers", "color")` and `get_texture("render_buffers", "depth")` access resolved attachments when requested; `get_texture("forward_clustered", "normal_roughness")` provides Forward+ geometry information. These are **engine textures**, not CPU arrays. The [compositor tutorial](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/rendering/compositor.html) says custom effects require Forward+ or Mobile; normal/roughness requires Forward+. `Viewport.get_texture()` followed by `Texture2D.get_image()` can capture display RGB, but invokes a readback path. Exact texture availability, normal/depth encoding, velocity convention, and callback timing require a Stage 1 probe against a pinned Godot release. Godot marks this API experimental.

Source-inspection starting points for that probe are Godot's [`servers/rendering/renderer_rd/renderer_scene_render_rd.cpp`](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/renderer_scene_render_rd.cpp) and [`servers/rendering/renderer_rd/storage_rd/render_scene_buffers_rd.cpp`](https://github.com/godotengine/godot/blob/master/servers/rendering/renderer_rd/storage_rd/render_scene_buffers_rd.cpp), plus the official demo's [`compute/post_shader/post_process_shader.gd`](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-demo-projects/blob/master/compute/post_shader/post_process_shader.gd). These `master` links are navigation references, not a pinned tested version. The normal/roughness texture has an [optimized encoding caveat](https://github.com/godotengine/godot-docs/issues/9591), reinforcing the need to verify decoding against the chosen release.

Our model belongs after ordinary shading has produced a cheap AO/lighting input and before tone mapping/presentation where possible. The **first** experiment instead exports the buffers offline. A custom AO pass must expose cheap and reference AO explicitly; Godot's built-in SSAO is not assumed to expose a standalone AO texture. The reference AO and reference RGB are training/evaluation targets only. At inference, use cheap AO, linearized depth, and view-space normals; motion is added only in the temporal stage.

## Scientific framing and risks

[Deep Shading](https://arxiv.org/abs/1603.06078) demonstrates screen-space CNNs conditioned on geometry attributes for shading effects. [NVIDIA NRD](https://github.com/NVIDIA-RTX/NRD) and [SVGF](https://research.nvidia.com/labs/rtr/publication/schied2017spatiotemporal/) show why geometry and temporal reprojection matter in denoising. [Intel ASSAO](https://www.intel.com/content/www/us/en/developer/articles/technical/adaptive-screen-space-ambient-occlusion.html) motivates a conventional quality/cost baseline. AO is a bounded first task, but screen-space AO cannot recover off-screen or fully occluded geometry; its high-sample version is an **algorithmic reference**, not physical truth. Godot's [environment docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/stable/tutorials/3d/environment_and_post_processing.html) describe this class of limitation.

Major risks: callback buffer availability/encoding; paired-frame mismatches from temporal post effects, random samples, or simulation state; screenshot/readback and format conversion costs; Core ML operator conversion; GPU contention on 8 GB unified memory; learned smoothing/hallucinated shadow detail; poor generalization to unseen geometry; and a neural path slower than a comparable conventional AO path. Each has a falsifiable checkpoint in the plan.

## MacBook Air M2 feasibility check (2026-11-11)

Observed locally: Mac14,2, Apple M2 with 8 GPU cores, 8 GB unified memory, Metal supported, macOS 26.5.1; Xcode is installed. Godot is **not** installed, so Godot execution and compositor behavior have not yet been tested on this machine. [Godot 4.7 system requirements](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/about/system_requirements.html) list Apple Silicon and 8 GB RAM for the editor, and its [rendering architecture docs](https://docs.godotengine.org/en/4.7/engine_details/architecture/internal_rendering_architecture.html) document native Metal with Forward+ on Apple Silicon. This supports **can launch a simple project**, not a guaranteed frame rate for our pipeline.

PyTorch 2.13.0 is installed. An MPS availability/convolution probe failed inside the restricted tool sandbox but **passed outside it**: `torch.backends.mps.is_available()` returned `True`, and a 5→16 channel convolution on a 1×5×144×256 MPS tensor produced shape 1×16×144×256. That verifies a basic GPU operation, not training throughput, memory headroom, or real-time inference. `coremltools` is absent and was not installed.

The strongest alternative is a tiny native Metal renderer using only simple original geometry. It is more work than Godot but has exact control over color, depth, normal, AO and timing, and avoids an engine-to-ML bridge for offline capture. Apple's [Metal render-texture documentation](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/textures) supports the attachment approach. Godot Mobile may render simple scenes faster, but Forward+ is favored because the documented compositor normal/roughness input is Forward+ specific. Godot Compatibility/OpenGL loses the needed RenderingDevice compositor path. The first offline experiment is therefore feasible in principle on this Mac; **live end-to-end FPS remains unverified** until a real project and model are profiled.
